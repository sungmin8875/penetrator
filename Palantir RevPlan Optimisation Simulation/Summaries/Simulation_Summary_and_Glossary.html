SCM Production Simulation — What It Is & Key Terms

A plain-language summary of the production capacity simulation for the Gumi substrate plant, plus a glossary of the manufacturing and supply-chain terms behind it.

Manufacturing · Substrate (기판) BU Oracle ERP Implementation phase Customer: LG Innotek

1 The big picture

The LG Innotek SCM team (substrate / 기판 business unit, Gumi plant) runs a production capacity simulation to answer one core question:

"If we commit to this production plan, can we actually make it — and will we hit our revenue target?"

Today they do this in a homegrown Excel file refined over 5+ years. It's accurate and trusted, but manual, slow, impossible to share (confidential), and must be re-run by hand every time the Move Plan changes. The earlier Palantir Foundry POC tried to systematize it but was never usable in practice — data wasn't fully connected, some logic was incomplete, and it was built for executives rather than the planners doing the work.

The Celonis project's job is to rebuild this simulation properly so practitioners can actually use it, with automatic runs, scenario variables, and outputs that lead to concrete decisions.

Source: 260609_LGI_인터뷰 노트_시뮤레이션 관련(SCM)

2 What it actually computes

At its core, the simulation is a load-vs-capacity check. It takes the demand plan, translates it into work at each manufacturing process step, and checks where that work exceeds equipment capacity:

Move Plan
  → apply model Routing
  → compute per-process load / demand
  → apply yield (수율)
  → compare against CAPA
  → identify bottlenecks
  → derive required actions

When load exceeds capacity, planners decide what to do: cut revenue, launch a productivity-improvement task, evaluate investment, or reallocate work between partners/factories. The point is that the result feeds a follow-up action — not just a dashboard.

3 The two simulations in your folder

These map directly to the two use cases discussed in the interview:

UC2-1 · Order Companion

Urgent Order Response

2-1.거급_오더_대응.ipynb

A WIP-lot allocation algorithm for emergency orders. ~30% of orders are urgent (~300 in revenue terms) and only ~50% get accepted today. With a ~90-day lead time, it asks: given lots already in progress, can we slot an urgent order in without breaking existing commitments? The pain isn't penalties — it's lost revenue and market share when orders must be declined.

UC2-2 · RevPlan Optimizer

Scenario-Based Simulation

2-2.시나리오_베이스_시뮤레이션.ipynb

The bigger engine: a Revenue-Driven Allocation Engine (수익 기반 용량 할당 욨진). It decides, for each production step, when to run it, on which equipment, and how much new production (Virtual Lots) to create to fill demand gaps. It processes month by month (202602 → 202603 → …), allocates capacity by model priority within each month, and carries unmet demand forward.

4 Constraints the simulation must honor

The simulation isn't a simple sum — it reflects several real-world rules the SCM team called out: Grouping Models (similar models grouped together, using average/median values for new models), Mix CAPA (special-process throughput depends on the mix of models running), prototype CAPA reservation (reserve ~10–20% of capacity for prototypes — an adjustable variable), equipment constraints (some equipment is blocked for certain models), and multi-factor priority (revenue is the #1 KPI, but material availability, lead time, margin, and customer priority all matter depending on the period).

5 Where they want it to go

The target is automatic execution whenever a new Move Plan or Business Plan drops (no manual re-runs), driven by both the short-term Move Plan (revenue achievement) and the longer-term Business Plan (investment / CAPA decisions), over a 1-year+ horizon, with user-adjustable scenario variables and outputs that lead to concrete follow-up actions (graphs, summary dashboard, Excel export). There's also appetite for AI on top — recommending similar grouping models, suggesting responses to new situations, and a copilot-style assistant.

⚠ Known risk: The trusted Excel logic could not be shared (confidential), and the interview note says a Gumi plant visit is required to capture the real logic. So the current notebooks reflect the reconstructed Palantir logic — not the validated Excel logic. Treat this gap as an open risk when assessing correctness.

6 Glossary — manufacturing & SCM terms

The terms you'll keep running into across the notebooks and interview notes:

TermWhat it means
CAPACapacity / 쮂파The maximum amount a process or piece of equipment can produce in a given period (e.g. sheets per day). The simulation's whole job is checking whether planned demand fits inside CAPA. When demand > CAPA, you have a bottleneck.
Grouping Model그룹픿 모했Rather than simulating every individual product model (there are too many, and new ones appear constantly), LGI groups models with similar routing and process characteristics and simulates the group using assumed/average values. New models inherit defaults (e.g. median lead time) from the closest existing group. Trades a little precision for a usable, maintainable model.
Mix CAPA믿스 쮂파For special processes, real throughput isn't a flat number — it depends on which models are running together and in what proportion. The same machine might process more of Model A and less of Model B. So capacity has to be computed from the model mix, not a single standard rate.
Lot로트A batch of product that moves through the manufacturing process together. The basic unit the simulation schedules and tracks.
WIPWork In Progress / 재공Lots already in progress on the line — partially completed work. The urgent-order engine (UC2-1) allocates against WIP to see if a new order can be accommodated without disrupting committed lots.
Virtual Lot가상 로트A "what-if" lot the engine creates to cover a demand shortfall. It doesn't exist yet on the line — it's the simulation proposing new production needed to meet a plan.
Process Step공정 단계A single operation in the manufacturing sequence (e.g. Drilling, AOI inspection). A model's full sequence of steps is its Routing.
Routing라우팅 / 공정 경로The defined sequence of process steps a given model must pass through to be built. The simulation applies a model's routing to turn demand into per-step workload.
Equipment Group설비 그룹A set of machines that can all perform the same operation. A process step is allocated to an equipment group rather than one fixed machine, giving the engine flexibility.
Yield수율The percentage of input that comes out as good product (the rest is scrap/rework). The sim applies yield so that to ship N good units you must start more than N — which raises the real load on each process.
Lead Time리드타임The total time to produce a product end-to-end. At LGI this is long — about 90 days, sometimes over 4 months — which is exactly why simulating capacity ahead of time matters.
Move Plan이동계획The short-term production/shipment plan aimed at hitting revenue. It's the primary trigger: the team wants the sim to run automatically every time the Move Plan changes.
Business Plan사업계획The longer-term plan used for investment and capacity decisions (and to secure funding). Revised continuously from ~July–August for the next year. The team wants this fed into the sim too.
Prototype CAPA시제품 쮂파A reserved slice of total capacity set aside for prototype/sample production (e.g. 10–20%), separate from mass-production CAPA. The reservation ratio is adjustable, so it's modeled as a simulation variable. The Excel had "with-prototype" and "without-prototype" versions of the load analysis.
Net Demand순 수요The net production quantity required per model per month — the demand the engine tries to fulfill before creating Virtual Lots for any shortfall.
Carryover이월Demand not met this month rolls into the next. Important nuance in the engine: carryover counts allocated units, not on-time completed units — once a lot is scheduled the demand is treated as covered, preventing duplicate lots for already-covered demand.
Target Month목표 월The month a given demand must be satisfied in. A final "ready-to-ship" step can be made to wait until the target month so the plant doesn't deliver early.
Allocation할당Assigning a lot's process step to a specific date and piece of equipment. The UC2-2 engine is fundamentally an allocation engine.
Fast-track패스트래트A mechanism letting high-priority models clear more process steps per day (e.g. up to 5 instead of the normal 2), so urgent/important demand moves through faster.
Bottleneck병목The process or equipment where required load exceeds capacity — the constraint that limits overall output and triggers a decision (reduce demand, add capacity, invest, reallocate).
OTDOn-Time Delivery / 납기준수율The rate at which orders are delivered by their promised date — a key supply-chain KPI raised in the RevPlan use case.
Unit conversionPNL · SHT · STR · EASubstrate quantities are tracked at several nested levels and converted between them. The notebook hierarchy is Sheets > Panels > Units; the interview also references PNL (panel) > SHT (sheet) > STR > EA (each). Capacity and demand must be converted to a common unit before they can be compared.
